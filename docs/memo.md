# Memo: Where to focus retention and delivery effort

**To:** E-commerce operations manager (hypothetical) **Date:** 2026-10-09
**Basis:** 96,478 delivered Olist orders, 93,358 customers, 2016-2018. Product revenue R$13.22M.

## Bottom line

Almost every customer buys once, and late deliveries come with sharply worse reviews. The data supports prioritizing a **tested** reactivation pilot and **delivery fixes in the northeast**. It does not support promising revenue gains from either.

## 1. Repeat purchasing is the largest gap

- Only 3.00% of customers (2,801) ever ordered twice; just 0.48% came back in the month after their first order.
- Two segments hold 62% of product revenue with 32% of customers. One of them, **High Value At Risk**, is 14,537 customers who spent R$4.05M historically and last bought about nine months or more ago.

**Recommendation:** run a controlled pilot on a sample of High Value At Risk customers, with a holdout group that gets no message, and compare reorder rates. Do not forecast recovered revenue: only 5.0% of this segment has ever reordered, so "at risk" means "bought long ago," not "likely to return."
**Caveat:** segments are quantile-based, and the data has no marketing history.

## 2. Late deliveries go with low reviews, and cluster geographically

- Late orders average 2.27 stars vs 4.29 for on-time or early orders; 6.77% of orders are late.
- Highest late rates: Alagoas 21.4%, Maranhão 17.4%, Sergipe 15.2%, Piauí 13.9%, Ceará 13.8%.

**Recommendation:** review estimated-delivery accuracy and carrier performance for these five states first, and request seller- and carrier-level data to find where delays arise.
**Caveats:** this is an association, not proof lateness lowers reviews. Late orders may differ in other ways. Even if the whole gap were due to lateness, the overall average would rise by only about 0.14 points. Smaller states (e.g. Alagoas, 397 orders) have wide uncertainty of roughly ±4 points.

## 3. Catalog data quality and category mix

- The top 5 categories are 39.8% of product revenue; no single category exceeds 9.4%, so concentration risk is moderate.
- 610 products (R$170,727, 1.3% of revenue) have no category, which weakens category reporting.

**Recommendation:** require a category at listing, and keep tracking the top-5 share as the catalog grows.

## What this analysis cannot tell you

No marketing, cost, margin, or seller-level data was available, so it cannot show profit, campaign effects, or why northeastern deliveries are slower. The data ends in August 2018.
